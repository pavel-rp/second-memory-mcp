## Executive Summary — 5 facts most decision-relevant to a monetization call

1. **No MCP-native payment rail exists yet — the closest thing (SEP‑2007, "Payment Support for MCP Servers") is still a draft proposal** created 2025‑12‑23 ([github.com/modelcontextprotocol/modelcontextprotocol](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/modelcontextprotocol/issues/2008)), and the MCP spec's own 2026‑07‑28 release candidate ships only two extensions (MCP Apps, Tasks) — no payments [T1, [blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28-release-candidate](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28-release-candidate/), verified 2026‑07‑07]. The production payment activity that does exist (x402, Stripe's Agentic Commerce Protocol) runs *alongside* MCP, governed separately (x402 Foundation under the Linux Foundation since April 2026), not inside it. One real production example of per-call charging exists today: **Apify's official MCP server** supports x402 (USDC/Base) and Skyfire tokens for per-run payment [T1, [github.com/apify/apify-mcp-server](https://github.com/apify/apify-mcp-server), verified 2026‑07‑07].

2. **Direct first-party overlap already shipped on the exact platform second-memory depends on.** Anthropic's **Claude Learning Mode** (Socratic-questioning tutor style) launched with Claude for Education in April 2025 ([anthropic.com/news/introducing-claude-for-education](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-claude-for-education)) and was expanded free to *all* Claude.ai users on 2025‑08‑14 [T2, [engadget.com](https://www.engadget.com/ai/anthropic-brings-claudes-learning-mode-to-regular-users-and-devs-170018471.html)]. **Claude Memory** (cross-session summarization) rolled out to all users, including free tier, starting ~2026‑03‑02, but is explicitly scoped to "claude.ai and the Claude app" — it does **not** apply to API access or Claude Code [T1, [support.claude.com/en/articles/11817273](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11817273-use-claude-s-chat-search-and-memory-to-build-on-previous-context), verified 2026‑07‑07]. Neither includes spaced-repetition scheduling or an explicit, queryable cross-session knowledge graph — that gap is the surviving differentiator, and it is narrow. (A widely-circulated claim that OpenAI *removed* ChatGPT's competing Study Mode around April 2026 is **weakly sourced** — a Hacker News thread ([news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47739305](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47739305)), an opinion blog, and one tweet, with no tier-1 press coverage or OpenAI statement found either way, and one source ([appscribed.com](https://appscribed.com/chatgpt-study-mode/)) claims it was still live in April 2026. Treat as "reported, unconfirmed" — do not anchor strategy on it.)

3. **The SRS niche shows real but narrow willingness-to-pay layered on a free core — and the flagship free product just changed hands.** AnkiHub charges $6–10/month for continuously-curated medical-deck content on top of the fully-free Anki engine [T1, [ankihub.net](https://www.ankihub.net/), verified 2026‑07‑07], with **zero public subscriber or revenue disclosure** anywhere (extensively searched, UNVERIFIED). More strikingly: Anki's sole 19‑year maintainer transferred business stewardship to AnkiHub's founders on 2026‑02‑02, citing burnout, with public commitments to keep the core free and open [T1, [forums.ankiweb.net/t/ankis-growing-up/68610](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/ankis-growing-up/68610), verified 2026‑07‑07] — i.e., the ecosystem is actively consolidating around a monetized-service-on-free-core shape, the same shape available to second-memory.

4. **Two positioning gaps are genuinely uncrowded — which cuts both ways.** (a) No dominant commercial product owns "AI tutor that remembers you across sessions + builds a knowledge graph of you" as its headline pitch; the closest matches are either unpriced/early ([Gradual Learning](https://graduallearning.com/)), narrow ([MathGPTPro](https://info.mathgptpro.com/), math-only), general memory infra pivoting into education as a vertical (Mem0, $24M YC-backed — [techcrunch.com/2025/10/28](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/28/mem0-raises-24m-from-yc-peak-xv-and-basis-set-to-build-the-memory-layer-for-ai-apps/)), or non-commercial research ([LOOM, arXiv 2511.21037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21037), Nov 2025; [DeepTutor](https://github.com/HKUDS/DeepTutor), OSS). (b) No self-serve, transparently-priced "spaced-repetition/knowledge-tracing-as-a-service" API exists either — SuperMemo's own developer API launched 2026‑03‑31 in early access with pricing explicitly "not yet final" [T1, [supermemo.com/en/blog/supermemo-api-launch](https://www.supermemo.com/en/blog/supermemo-api-launch-the-spaced-repetition-algorithm-is-now-available-to-developers), verified 2026‑07‑07]. Absence of a competitor is not evidence of demand — in both cases, nobody has yet proven anyone will pay for this specific bundle.

5. **Open-core cloud add-ons for small OSS infra projects show a real but bimodal outcome — and one of the strongest precedents just reversed course.** Plausible Analytics (AGPLv3 since 2020) is bootstrapped, profitable, reports its best-ever month for new subscribers in April 2026, and self-reports "19K paying subscribers" on its own homepage [T1/self-reported, [plausible.io](https://plausible.io/), verified 2026‑07‑07]. But **Cal.com went fully closed-source on 2026‑04‑14/17**, citing AI-driven vulnerability-scanning risk, spinning off a deliberately feature-stripped MIT fork (`cal.diy`, confirmed via direct GitHub API check) and triggering a Hacker News backlash calling it a "bait-and-switch" [T1, [cal.com/blog/cal-com-goes-closed-source-why](https://cal.com/blog/cal-com-goes-closed-source-why), [itsfoss.com/news/cal-com-goes-proprietary](https://itsfoss.com/news/cal-com-goes-proprietary/), verified 2026‑07‑07] — the reverse of the open-core playbook. Separately, purpose-built paid clouds bolted onto healthy OSS projects have quietly died while the OSS core lived on under the same maintainer: **Locust Cloud** shut down ~Dec 2025 ("we simply weren't able to attract enough frequent users" — [github.com/locustio/locust/pull/3314](https://github.com/locustio/locust/pull/3314)) and **Sandstorm Oasis** shut down in 2019 for the same reason [T1, [sandstorm.io/news/2019-09-15-shutting-down-oasis](https://sandstorm.io/news/2019-09-15-shutting-down-oasis), verified 2026‑07‑07].

### A2. Anthropic connectors directory & the official MCP Registry
- The **official MCP Registry** (registry.modelcontextprotocol.io) is explicitly in **preview, not GA**; API freeze for v0.1 announced 2025‑10‑24 [T1, github.com/modelcontextprotocol/registry, verified 2026‑07‑07]. It is a self-service **metaregistry** — namespace-authenticated (GitHub OAuth/OIDC or DNS proof), storing metadata only (actual code lives on npm/PyPI/Docker Hub). **No payment, billing, or monetization mechanism of any kind appears in it.**
- **Anthropic's Connectors Directory** is a separate, curated surface: requires proof of ownership of the API/domain/resources touched, OAuth 2.0, HTTPS, a public privacy policy, a pre-populated test account for reviewers, and submission through a portal that requires a **Team or Enterprise** Claude.ai org (individual accounts cannot submit) [T2, claude.com/docs/connectors/building/submission, verified 2026‑07‑07]. Anthropic does not publish a review-time SLA; community reports range from two weeks to several months [T2].
- **No revenue-share or payment-processing rail is offered by Anthropic to connector developers** — checked the (now-consolidated) Connectors Directory FAQ directly; no monetization mechanism mentioned [T1, support.claude.com, verified 2026‑07‑07].
- **UNVERIFIED** — official Anthropic-published traffic/install volume for the directory or any individual connector. The only counts found (343–554 "tracked integrations") come from an **unofficial third-party GitHub tracker**, not Anthropic itself, and a widely-repeated "97 million Claude SDK downloads/month" figure could not be confirmed as connector-install volume specifically — it traces to a third-party stats blog describing general SDK/package downloads. **Do not cite either number as a fact.**

### A3. Does ChatGPT support third-party MCP servers for consumers in 2026?
- **Yes, with a tier split that matters strategically.** OpenAI launched "apps in ChatGPT" (built on MCP) at DevDay, 2025‑10‑06 [T2, TechCrunch/openai.com], then opened an in-ChatGPT **App Directory** to third-party submissions on 2025‑12‑17/19, renaming "connectors" to "apps" [T1, VentureBeat, published 2025‑12‑19].
- Per OpenAI's own current Apps SDK docs: **curated, reviewed apps are available to all logged-in ChatGPT users — Free, Go, Plus, Pro — outside the EEA, Switzerland, and the UK** [T1, developers.openai.com/apps-sdk, verified 2026‑07‑07]. Only **"Developer Mode"** — registering *any* arbitrary MCP server yourself, including write-capable actions — is gated to **Business, Enterprise, and Edu** plans [T1, help.openai.com (search-snippet corroborated; help.openai.com blocks automated fetch), verified 2026‑07‑07]. This means second-memory could in principle reach ChatGPT's consumer base as a reviewed app, not just enterprise Developer Mode users — but only after OpenAI's review, and subject to (below) its monetization limits.
- **Revenue-share: none disclosed, and digital-goods monetization isn't approved yet.** OpenAI's Apps SDK monetization guide states developers are "responsible for choosing how to monetize" via **external checkout** (recommended default) or **in-app checkout with saved payment methods**; the **"ChatGPT payment sheet" is private beta**, "limited to select marketplaces," and **current approval is limited to physical-goods purchases** — subscriptions/digital goods are not yet approved [T1, developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/build/monetization, verified 2026‑07‑07]. No revenue-share percentage is disclosed anywhere in the docs. (A distinct, unrelated number: OpenAI's Shopify/Etsy "Instant Checkout" commerce partnership reportedly takes ~4% of sale value [T2] — that's e-commerce checkout, not general app monetization.)

### A4. Agent-native payment rails — production status mid‑2026
- **Stripe/OpenAI Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP)**: open spec, GitHub status badge marked **"beta"**; latest stable spec version 2026‑04‑17 [T1, github.com/agentic-commerce-protocol, verified 2026‑07‑07]. **Five named production deployments as of April 2026**: ChatGPT Instant Checkout, Amazon "Buy for Me," Mastercard Agent Pay, Visa Intelligent Commerce, Coinbase Agent.market [T2, agenticplug.ai tracker]. This is checkout/commerce infrastructure (buying goods), not a per-tool-call metering mechanism.
- **Stripe's Machine Payments Protocol (MPP)** — a fiat-rails alternative to x402 for session-based agent billing — opened to developers March 2026 [T2].
- **x402** (Coinbase-originated HTTP‑402 micropayment protocol): governed since April 2026 by the **x402 Foundation under the Linux Foundation** (members include Coinbase, AWS, Cloudflare, Anthropic, Circle) [T2, blockeden.xyz]. Coinbase/Chainalysis **self-report** "over 169 million payments across 590,000 buyers and 100,000 sellers" in its first operational year [T2, chainalysis.com — **Coinbase-adjacent, self-reported, not independently audited**]. **AWS CloudFront/WAF shipped x402 support to GA ~2026‑06‑15**; **Cloudflare's "Monetization Gateway" is waitlist-only** as of 2026‑07‑06 [T1, infoq.com, published 2026‑07‑06].
- **An MCP-native payments extension is still only a draft.** GitHub issue **SEP‑2007 "Payment Support for MCP Servers"**, status **Draft**, created 2025‑12‑23, proposes x402 v2 as the first supported method with a JSON‑RPC `-32402` payment-required error code [T1, github.com/modelcontextprotocol/modelcontextprotocol, verified 2026‑07‑07]. It did not make the 2026‑07‑28 release candidate.
- **Concrete production example of an MCP server charging per call**: Apify's official MCP server (x402/USDC + Skyfire) [T1, verified above]. Coinbase's **"x402 Bazaar"** claims to index "10,000+ existing paid MCP tools" — **self-reported by Coinbase**, not independently verified [T2].
- **UNVERIFIED** — any individual MCP server operator's actual real-money revenue collected via x402/ACP/MPP; no per-operator disclosure found anywhere.

### A5. Platform risk: first-party learning/memory overlap, and historical base rate
- **Claude Learning Mode** (Socratic questioning, guides rather than answers): launched with Claude for Education 2025‑04‑02 [T1, anthropic.com/news], expanded free to all Claude.ai users 2025‑08‑14 [T2, Engadget, Dataconomy]. **This directly overlaps with second-memory's "Socratic tutor" positioning** — but does not include spaced-repetition scheduling or a persistent, queryable cross-session knowledge graph.
- **Claude Memory**: rolled out to all users including free tier starting ~2026‑03‑02 [T2, aggregated third-party trackers — moderate confidence, not an Anthropic primary date independently fetched]. Explicitly, per Anthropic's own help documentation: **"Claude memory is a claude.ai and Claude app feature — it does not apply to API access or Claude Code"** [T1, support.claude.com, verified 2026‑07‑07]. Mechanism is a ~24-hour-refreshed chat-history *summary*, not explicit retrieval-practice scheduling or graph-structured mastery tracking — genuinely different mechanism from second-memory's core loop, though it competes for the same "Claude remembers me" mindshare.
- **Claude for Education** (institutional program): bundles Learning Mode, Canvas LMS integration, Wiley research-content access; partner institutions in 2026 include Stanford (all faculty/students/staff from 2026‑06‑30), LSE, Northeastern, Dartmouth, plus a CodePath partnership reaching 20,000+ students [T1, anthropic.com/news/advancing-claude-for-education, verified 2026‑07‑07].
- **ChatGPT Study Mode**: shipped, confirmed via OpenAI's own announcement (Socratic questioning + scaffolded responses, uses ChatGPT Memory when enabled) [T2, openai.com/index/chatgpt-study-mode]. **Reported removal ~2026‑04‑12–18 is weakly sourced**: a Hacker News "Tell HN" thread (189 pts/78 comments), an opinion blog (agent-wars.com), and a tweet from Ethan Mollick — **no tier-1 press coverage and no OpenAI statement found either confirming or denying it**, and one aggregator (appscribed.com) claims it was still available on all plans as of April 2026. **Treat as unconfirmed; do not use as a headline platform-risk anchor.**
- **Historical base rate (non‑AI, well-sourced, for calibration)**: Twitter cut off third-party client API access Jan–Feb 2023, a documented deliberate move to eliminate ecosystem apps that didn't serve ads [T1, TechCrunch, 2023‑01‑16 & 2023‑02‑01]. Facebook's May 2012 Platform changes deprioritized app discovery, contributing to Zynga's collapse from a $14.50 IPO to under $3 by 2013 [T1, Wharton, verified 2026‑07‑07]. **No documented AI-platform-specific case yet exists** of a first-party feature demonstrably killing a specific MCP-based competitor — UNVERIFIED/no case found, searched extensively.

### C10. Learning/memory infrastructure sold as an API
- **SuperMemo API**: officially launched 2026‑03‑31, explicitly **"in an early access stage,"** free tier 100 repetitions/day, one-time historical import up to 10,000 repetitions, and **"transactions have not yet been enabled, and pricing is not yet final"** [T1, supermemo.com/en/blog, verified 2026‑07‑07].
- **Domoscio Spark API**: public developer docs exist for a spaced-repetition/adaptive-learning integration API, but **no pricing appears anywhere** on the fetched documentation [T1, developers.domoscio.com, verified 2026‑07‑07].
- **Knewton (Wiley)** dev portal: targets partner-integration developers, but page footer reads "Copyright © 2021," suggesting stale/unmaintained docs even though the underlying service's status page shows no incidents in June 2026; no pricing published [T1, dev.knewton.com, verified 2026‑07‑07].
- **Riiid → Socra AI**: Riiid rebranded to Socra AI in September 2025; its "R.Inside" AI-as-a-service offering's own URL chain now 404s — **unclear if it still exists as a distinct, separately-priced product** [T1 for the 404, T2 for the rebrand].
- **Realizeit/Skillwell**: merged; the official `/subscribe` page 301-redirects to a generic marketing page with no pricing shown [T1, verified 2026‑07‑07].
- **FSRS / Open Spaced Repetition**: not a company — a **free, open-source** scheduling algorithm implemented across multiple languages [T1, github.com/open-spaced-repetition, verified 2026‑07‑07]. Relevant as a competitive ceiling: the core scheduling math itself is already a free commodity, which structurally limits how much any commercial API layer can charge for scheduling alone.

**Category-level finding**: a self-serve, transparently-priced "spaced-repetition-as-a-service" or "knowledge-tracing API" — i.e., a Stripe/Twilio-style developer product with a public price list — **does not exist as of mid‑2026**. Every player found is either pre-revenue/early-access with pricing undecided (SuperMemo), enterprise-sales-dark with no public number (Domoscio, Knewton, Skillwell), of uncertain current status (Riiid/Socra), or a free algorithm with no monetizing company at all (FSRS). This is itself the finding — treat it as an absence of demand evidence, not as blue-ocean opportunity (see Implications, model 4).

## Implications — Scoring the Candidate Models

**Rubric** (each axis scored 1–5, where **5 is always the more favorable outcome for pursuing that model**):
- **Demand evidence** — 5 = a directly comparable product proves people pay for *this specific thing*; 1 = no such proof exists anywhere.
- **Achievable pricing** — 5 = strong comps show a healthy, defensible price point; 1 = comps show pricing pressure toward zero or no viable price found at all.
- **Competition** — 5 = little to no direct competitor; 1 = brutally crowded/commoditized.
- **Platform risk (safety)** — 5 = low risk that Anthropic/OpenAI absorb this into a free first-party feature; 1 = high risk / already overlapping today.

**A note on how to read this, before the numbers**: demand evidence is a gate, not one input among four to be added up. A model with no proof anyone will pay for it is not "one notch worse" than a model with strong proof — it is not yet a viable business, regardless of how favorable its competition or platform-risk scores look. Two rows below (content marketplace, learning-infra API) score reasonably on competition and platform-safety *precisely because* no one has tried and failed yet, not because the ground is fertile. Read demand first; treat the other three as modifiers only once demand clears a minimum bar.

### 1. Consumer hosted SaaS (individual subscription, freemium tiers)
- Demand evidence: **3/5** — Strong *adjacent* proof (AnkiHub $6–10/mo for curated SR content; Duolingo Max $168/yr and RemNote $8–18/mo for AI-assisted learning tools at real scale), but no product has proven people pay specifically for the SR + knowledge-graph + LLM-tutor bundle second-memory offers.
- Achievable pricing: **4/5** — The competitive band ($5–20/mo across SRS and AI-tutor products, up to Duolingo Max's $29.99/mo) brackets a defensible $8–15/mo consumer price point.
- Competition: **3/5** — Crowded at each individual layer (SRS: Anki ecosystem, RemNote, Mochi, SuperMemo; AI tutoring: Khanmigo, Synthesis, Duolingo Max) but the specific intersection is open — no one product does both.
- Platform risk: **2/5** — The highest-risk row in this table. Claude Learning Mode (Socratic tutoring) and Claude Memory (cross-session recall) already ship free, on the exact platform this product depends on for its own client-side inference. The surviving differentiator — explicit SR scheduling plus a queryable knowledge graph — is real but narrow, and Anthropic has shown no roadmap commitment either way (UNVERIFIED whether it stays uncontested).

### 2. B2B teams (sell into corporate onboarding/compliance/L&D)
- Demand evidence: **3/5** — [Haekka's](https://www.haekka.com/pricing) $1–2/employee/mo for Slack-embedded compliance training is a concrete, verified proof point that "learning inside the tool you already use" is a real paid category; the [$400B L&D TAM figure](https://joshbersin.com/2026/02/new-research-how-ai-transforms-400-billion-of-corporate-learning/) is context, not proof, for this specific product angle.
- Achievable pricing: **3/5** — Per-seat comps range from $8/user/mo (360Learning) to enterprise contracts with no public ceiling, but most of the category (Docebo, WorkRamp, Sana, Arist) is contact-sales-only, meaning pricing is negotiated per deal rather than self-evidently achievable at a stated number.
- Competition: **2/5** — Crowded incumbents (Docebo, 360Learning, WorkRamp, Sana, Arist, Uplimit, Disco) plus a structurally dangerous free bundling threat: Microsoft Viva Learning is already built into Teams by default, and the Microsoft 365 Copilot "Learning Agent" is reported GA in 2026 — a much larger platform bundling learning for free into infrastructure most target companies already pay for.
- Platform risk: **3/5** — The dominant bundling threat here is Microsoft, not Anthropic/OpenAI, so it's less directly tied to second-memory's own MCP-host dependency, but it is arguably an equally serious commercial threat by a different name.

### 3. Open-core cloud (stay MIT/self-hostable, sell hosted multi-tenant convenience)
- Demand evidence: **4/5** — The best-evidenced row. [Plausible](https://plausible.io/) (AGPLv3, self-reports 19K paying subscribers and its [best-ever growth month](https://plausible.io/blog/homepage-edits-conversion-lift) in April 2026) is close to a direct structural comp: free, self-hostable core; paid convenience for people who don't want to run their own Postgres+Node process — exactly second-memory's stated economics.
- Achievable pricing: **4/5** — Comps ([Plausible](https://plausible.io/) $9–19/mo entry tiers, [Umami](https://umami.is/pricing) $20/mo, [n8n](https://n8n.io/pricing/) $20–50/mo, [Typebot](https://typebot.com/pricing) $39–89/mo) show healthy, sustained pricing for hosted convenience alone, no content or inference cost required.
- Competition: **5/5** — No direct competitor runs an open-core hosted spaced-repetition-tutor MCP server today.
- Platform risk: **4/5** — Structurally the safest row against Anthropic/OpenAI specifically: the value being sold is hosting/multi-tenancy convenience and the user's own accumulated data/graph, not a chat-surface feature a model provider could trivially clone into their own product.
- **Necessary caveat, not a separate score**: this model's real risk is execution and license-strategy risk, not platform risk. [Cal.com abandoned open-core entirely in April 2026](https://cal.com/blog/cal-com-goes-closed-source-why) (closed-sourced its commercial code, citing AI-driven vulnerability scanning, and drew a public ["bait-and-switch" backlash](https://itsfoss.com/news/cal-com-goes-proprietary/) for it) — a live example of even a well-funded open-core company retreating from the model. Separately, [Locust Cloud](https://github.com/locustio/locust/pull/3314) and [Sandstorm Oasis](https://sandstorm.io/news/2019-09-15-shutting-down-oasis) both show that a bolted-on paid cloud can simply fail to find a market and quietly shut down while the OSS core lives on under the same maintainer, unharmed. On license mechanics specifically: **MIT alone (second-memory's current license) gives zero legal barrier to someone cloning the hosted service** — the two demonstrated small-operator playbooks are (a) AGPL plus *deliberate, separate* feature-gating of the cloud product ([Plausible's own words](https://github.com/plausible/analytics/discussions/3442): a "protective barrier around our cloud offering," maintained even though the AGPL license nominally covers the whole repo), or (b) a "fair-source" license with an explicit anti-competing-hosting clause (n8n's [Sustainable Use License](https://github.com/n8n-io/n8n/blob/master/LICENSE.md), Outline's BSL, Typebot's [FSL](https://www.typebot.com/blog/typebot-is-now-fair-source)) — each of which trades legal protection for real community-relations friction (visible in Typebot's own admission that AGPL "created confusion," and starkly in the Cal.com backlash).

### 4. Learning-infrastructure API (SR-as-a-service / knowledge-tracing API for other developers)
- Demand evidence: **1/5** — No self-serve, transparently-priced product exists in this category at all. [SuperMemo's own developer API](https://www.supermemo.com/en/blog/supermemo-api-launch-the-spaced-repetition-algorithm-is-now-available-to-developers), the most credible entrant, launched in early access on 2026‑03‑31 with pricing explicitly "not yet final." Absence of a productized market with public buyers is absence of demand evidence, not validated whitespace.
- Achievable pricing: **2/5** — No market pricing signal exists to anchor against, and the core scheduling algorithm (FSRS) is already a free, open-source commodity — which caps how much anyone can charge for scheduling math alone, independent of what wrapper/API convenience is added.
- Competition: **4/5** — Genuinely few players (SuperMemo early-access, Domoscio, a stale Knewton portal, an uncertain-status Riiid/Socra offering) — but low competition here coexists with low demand; it is uncontested because unproven, not because it's fertile ground.
- Platform risk: **5/5** — Neither Anthropic nor OpenAI has shown any indication of building a knowledge-tracing API product for third-party developers; this is the furthest row from either platform's roadmap.

### 5. Content marketplace (paid, creator-compensated shared decks/knowledge graphs)
- Demand evidence: **1/5** — Actively negative precedent, not merely absent: Anki's shared decks are free with zero creator-payment mechanism (confirmed directly in Anki's own contributor docs); AnkiHub itself is a subscription-access product, not a creator marketplace, and a free alternative (AnkiCollab) exists specifically positioned *against* AnkiHub's paid model; Quizlet monetizes its enormous UGC library entirely via its own ads/subscriptions with no creator-payment program found anywhere.
- Achievable pricing: **2/5** — No working comparable exists for charging end-users specifically for marketplace content, as distinct from a bundled platform subscription.
- Competition: **3/5** — Not crowded, but again because it's unproven rather than open ground.
- Platform risk: **4/5** — Low direct platform-overlap risk, but close to moot given the demand finding above.

### 6. Per-call micropayments (x402/HTTP‑402-style metering of individual tool calls)
- Demand evidence: **2/5** — Real production volume exists in the broader x402 ecosystem (Coinbase/Chainalysis self-report 169M+ payments across 590K+ buyers in its first operational year — self-reported, not independently audited), and Apify's MCP server proves per-call charging is technically live for at least one MCP tool. But zero evidence exists that anyone would pay per-call specifically for a tutoring interaction.
- Achievable pricing: **2/5** — Second-memory's own stated economics (near-zero marginal cost per call, since the client's LLM does the teaching) make per-call pricing *technically* feasible at almost any price point, but a tutor is a recurring, high-context relationship, not a one-shot metered tool call — the product shape is a poor fit for metering regardless of rail maturity, and a flat subscription almost certainly captures more of the value created per user than metering near-zero-cost calls would.
- Competition: **4/5** — Very few MCP servers charge per call today (Apify is the cleanest example found) — uncrowded.
- Platform risk: **3/5** — Moderate: not a first-party-overlap risk so much as a protocol-maturity risk — the MCP spec itself has no native payments extension yet (SEP‑2007 is still draft), so building a business on this rail today means building on infrastructure the standards body hasn't finished.

### Bottom line
Ranked by where the **evidence** (not an arithmetic total) points: **open-core cloud** is the best-supported model — closest structural comp (Plausible), healthiest achievable pricing, least direct platform-overlap exposure — provisioned that the license-strategy lesson from section D is taken seriously (MIT alone won't protect a hosted business; pair it with deliberate feature-gating or accept the community-friction cost of a fair-source license). **Consumer hosted SaaS** is the second most defensible path on demand and pricing grounds, but carries the highest platform risk in this table because Anthropic already ships adjacent features for free on the same surface. **B2B teams** has one concrete proof point (Haekka) but faces both incumbent and platform-bundling competition. **Per-call micropayments** and **learning-infra API** are technically uncontested but for a reason — no one has shown anyone will pay for either yet, and the product itself (a relationship, not a metered call; a proven-only-in-early-access category) fits poorly regardless. **Content marketplace** is the weakest row: the closest analog ecosystem (Anki/AnkiHub) has actively rejected paying creators for years.
