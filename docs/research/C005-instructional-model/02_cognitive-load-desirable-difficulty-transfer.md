# Cognitive Load · Desirable Difficulty · Transfer — Feature-Wide Framing

**Task:** NEU-915 · **Cutoff:** 2026-07-13
This file authors the feature-wide framing that every downstream mechanism sub-task applies. It is **evidence, not decision**: it states what the literature establishes about managing load, calibrating difficulty, and expecting transfer, and it fixes the vocabulary the per-mechanism files (`mechanisms/M01…M10`) reuse. It chooses no mechanism behavior.

Each mechanism file references this file for its "cognitive-load / desirable-difficulty note" and its "DP-transfer uncertainty." Reading this once means a downstream agent does not re-derive the framing ten times.

## 1. Cognitive load — the capacity constraint every mechanism spends against

**F-CL-1** — `[literature]` · Human working memory is sharply capacity-limited; instructional design quality rises when it respects that limit. Miller's foundational finding is that people hold about "seven plus or minus two units" of information in short-term memory, and the core instructional premise is that "the quality of instructional design will be raised if greater consideration is given to the role and limitations of working memory." *Provenance:* https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_load (secondary summary of Miller 1956 and Sweller's cognitive load theory). *Cutoff:* 2026-07-13. *Evidence type:* theoretical/foundational synthesis. *Limitation:* The exact capacity number is contested (Cowan's later "~4 chunks" estimate is commonly cited but was not independently fetched in this task — carried UNVERIFIED); the principle is well-established, the precise limit is not load-bearing for this synthesis.

**F-CL-2** — `[literature]` · Cognitive load theory partitions load into three kinds a mechanism decision must trade off. *Intrinsic* load is "the effort associated with a specific topic" (the inherent difficulty of the material — for DP, the recurrence/state/transition structure itself). *Extraneous* load is "the way information or tasks are presented to a learner" (avoidable burden from poor design). *Germane* load is "the work put into creating a permanent store of knowledge (a schema)" (the productive effort of learning). *Provenance:* https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_load. *Cutoff:* 2026-07-13. *Evidence type:* theoretical framework. *Limitation:* The germane/intrinsic distinction is theory-internal and has been debated in the CLT literature; the framework is a lens, not a measured quantity, and no per-learner load measurement exists in this project.

**Framing rule for mechanisms (evidence-derived, not a decision):** Every mechanism decision must state (a) how it manages **intrinsic** load (e.g. chunking DP concepts, prerequisite ordering), (b) where it removes **extraneous** load (e.g. worked examples cutting means-ends search), and (c) where it deliberately spends **germane** load / preserves desirable difficulty (e.g. retrieval, spacing). A mechanism asserted in isolation, without saying which load it spends or spares, is under-specified — this is a completeness requirement inherited from the NEU-888 charter, recorded here as the framing the mechanism files apply.

## 2. Desirable difficulty — the calibration axis

**F-DD-1** — `[literature]` · A *desirable difficulty* is effortful practice that improves long-term retention/transfer, but only within a bound. "A desirable difficulty is a learning task that requires a considerable but desirable amount of effort, thereby improving long-term performance," with the explicit caveat that "the task must be able to be accomplished. Too difficult a task may dissuade the learner and prevent full processing." *Provenance:* https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desirable_difficulty (reused via `docs/research/results/03-pedagogy-evidence-audit.md` Q11, cutoff 2026-07-07). *Cutoff:* 2026-07-07. *Evidence type:* causal (the underlying retrieval/spacing/interleaving manipulations are experimental) framed as a principle. *Limitation:* "Desirable" is defined relative to learner state; there is no domain-general threshold for when difficulty stops being desirable, and none is measured for DP.

**F-DD-2** — `[literature]` · The learning gain from a retrieval attempt is largest exactly when retrieval strength is low — "the lower the retrieval strength at that moment, the greater the boost in storage strength (i.e., learning)" — *provided the attempt succeeds*. This is the tension every difficulty-adapting mechanism sits inside: lowering difficulty (e.g. to recognition) protects success but forfeits the largest potential gain. *Provenance:* https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2016/5/10-1 (Bjork, reused via audit Q11). *Cutoff:* 2026-07-07. *Evidence type:* mechanistic/theoretical (new theory of disuse). *Limitation:* The "if it succeeds" condition is the whole design problem; the blog is a synthesis, not a single controlled study.

**F-DD-3** — `[literature]` · There is an optimal, learner-state-dependent difficulty band, not a single hardest-possible target. Metcalfe's *region of proximal learning* is "where a small amount of time and effort should yield maximal learning gains ... items perceived to be close to being learned but are not yet thought to have been mastered"; the Challenge Point Framework (motor-learning) generalizes the inverted-U: "learning is low when functional difficulty is too low or too high." *Provenance:* https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2742428/ (Metcalfe) and Guadagnoli & Lee via audit Q11 (Challenge Point, search-synthesis, not directly fetched — carried as directional). *Cutoff:* 2026-07-07. *Evidence type:* Metcalfe causal/experimental; Challenge Point motor-learning, transfer to conceptual DP unestablished. *Limitation:* Challenge Point is from motor learning; its application to conceptual problem-solving is an analogy.

**Framing rule for mechanisms:** Where a mechanism can dial difficulty (retrieval tier, hint depth, worked-example fading, interleaving degree), the decision must state where it deliberately *preserves* desirable difficulty and where it *removes* it to keep the task accomplishable, rather than defaulting to easiest-that-passes or hardest-possible.

## 3. Transfer — the standing, unmeasured uncertainty

**F-TR-1** — `[literature]` · Far transfer — applying a skill to contexts differing substantially in surface, structure, or context — is empirically hard and rare; a single effect size for it is not even well-defined. Barnett & Ceci's taxonomy uses nine dimensions and concludes that "estimation of a single effect size for far transfer is misguided in view of this complexity," with observed transfer in reviewed studies being *near* on most dimensions. *Provenance:* https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12081085/ (Barnett & Ceci 2002). *Cutoff:* 2026-07-13. *Evidence type:* review/taxonomy. *Limitation:* A 2002 taxonomy; it structures the problem rather than resolving transfer rates for any specific domain.

**F-TR-2** — `[literature]` · Retention-optimized mechanics (spacing, retrieval on facts) do not automatically produce problem-solving transfer; recent work treats far transfer of retrieval-practice benefits as achievable only through specific mechanisms (e.g. rule-based learning), not as a default. *Provenance:* Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications (2024), "Far transfer of retrieval-practice benefits: rule-based learning as the underlying mechanism," https://cognitiveresearchjournal.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s41235-024-00598-y (abstract-level; full text was auth-gated in this task, so the mechanism claim is carried as directional, the effect magnitude UNVERIFIED). *Cutoff:* 2026-07-13. *Evidence type:* empirical (magnitude UNVERIFIED here). *Limitation:* Not measured on dynamic programming; full text not fetched.

**F-TR-3 (inherited controlling uncertainty)** — `[literature]`/inherited-risk · The entire mechanism-level evidence base is studied largely on facts, category learning, and well-structured problems — **not** on dynamic-programming problem-solving. Its extension to DP is an analogy, not a measurement (NEU-887 risk R1 / conflict X1). *Provenance:* `docs/research/C005-product-foundation/README.md` (R1, non-downgradable High) and this synthesis. *Cutoff:* 2026-07-13. *Evidence type:* inherited risk. *Limitation:* This is the single largest standing gap; **every** mechanism file repeats it, and no mechanism may present DP-domain effectiveness as established.

**Framing rule for mechanisms:** Each mechanism file carries a one-line DP-transfer statement asserting that its cited effects are unmeasured in DP and that DP effectiveness stays provisional. Transfer is a per-mechanism *uncertainty*, never a per-mechanism *result*.

## 4. How the three axes interact (the framing later sub-tasks reconcile)

The three axes pull against each other, and that tension is the substance later sub-tasks must resolve — this synthesis only names it:

- **Load vs. difficulty.** Worked examples cut extraneous/means-ends load for novices (§M02), but the same support becomes redundant and *harmful* for more expert learners (expertise reversal, §M02) — so "reduce load" and "preserve desirable difficulty" are the same dial read at different expertise levels.
- **Difficulty vs. transfer.** Desirable difficulties reliably improve *retention*; whether they improve *DP problem-solving transfer* is exactly the unmeasured question (F-TR-2/3). A mechanism can be retention-optimal and transfer-neutral.
- **This feature's fixed audience** — mastery-oriented programmers who ultimately need competitive-programming breadth *and speed* — means the durable-mastery-vs-contest-speed tension (NEU-888 OUT-3) rides on top of all three axes. This synthesis supplies the evidence; the staged-or-measured resolution is a downstream decision, not made here.
